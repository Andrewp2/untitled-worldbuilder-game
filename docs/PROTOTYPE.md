# Movement, construction, combat, and world map milestones

The user approved an original game inspired by the reference, with real-time movement and handcrafted levels. The first milestone used simple geometric art; the approved toy-world redesign now supplies the current visuals. DESIGN.md preserves the gameplay discussion and records the current visual system. These are deliberate prototype choices, not assertions about the original game's exact rules.

Thirty-six ordered missions across Meadow Isles, Sunstone Range and Open Sea use the released roster's 20 buildable roles and six hostile roles. [ROSTER.md](ROSTER.md) records their original counterparts and authored adaptations; [CAMPAIGN.md](CAMPAIGN.md) lists the twelve distinct challenges in each world and their verification status. Hauler, Bristleback, and Signal relay remain separate additions; cut/sequel units are excluded. Recipes, statistics, and maps are authored adaptations. Only Hollow Reach starts unlocked; every preceding main is required for later access, while bonuses never gate progress.

## Surface and controls

The board fills the desktop viewport. A compact bottom toolbox contains always-visible pictured blueprints, selected name/charge and legal pictured actions; a short objective and game menu sit above. Remaining stock is visible, exhausted choices are disabled, and recipe costs appear on hover/focus/selection. Units are selected on the board or with 1–9. Installed charge uses one green bar with exact charge on hover and for assistive technology; battery replacement appears when available. Empty cargo slots and redundant status symbols are hidden. The mission information panel is removed; unit details and How to play remain available. Main delivery requirements use pictured colors and battery counts; mission-specific hints appear only inside How to play. Typography remains bundled Nunito Sans. Mobile reflow is outside the current scope.

The [approved study](../.impeccable/mocks/toy-world-study.png) guides friendly toy silhouettes, colors and materials rather than exact layout. All current unit/enemy roles, batteries, rocks, trees and colored parts are authored geometry in `src/view/toy-models.ts`, rendered once with a 30-degree orthographic camera matching the 80×40 grid. World textures are 384×384 with planted origin (192,280), displaying at 96px for rovers/Bristleback/rocks/trees. HUD portraits crop transparent margins. Trees use six broad rounded canopy lobes and warm trunks in the same camera and lighting as the rovers. Both tree sizes share the planted origin. The six-legged Bristleback uses one segmented red model for all four headings. Loose and installed batteries share the blue cylinder, collars and cap geometry; empty-cell tint and charge strips remain. Flags, relay, dirt and connector retain generated cutouts. The [manifest](../public/art/toy-world/manifest.json) is a provenance inventory of 34 retained PNGs, including superseded model/terrain/freighter references. The roster extension adds no shipped raster assets. New boat hulls fit one tile. No original reference code or art was copied.

Grass, sand and water use quiet broad color planes as a reversible trial; the user has not chosen a final terrain style. Water is 10 world pixels below land tops. Land sides use 10px depth, including the banks beside bridge ends. Bridge decks are 4px thick, with support posts and water underneath. Ripples render below the deck and posts. Rock bases stay flat and their entire support hull fits the blocked cell. Shadows, body depth and picking use planted footprints. Terrain, shadows, bars, marks and native action SVG remain code art.

Red, blue, yellow and green parts each have their own quantity, recipe meaning, pile picture and hover count. The same color pictures appear in costs and loaded cargo. Physical blue batteries preserve individual charge; brown dirt fills Scoop's bucket. The installed battery remains the sole power/health gauge. Foreground props occlude body and carried contents together; cargo positions project from the actual rendered bed/bucket.

Four views follow facing. Warden, Arborbot and Mender use four jointed walking legs; vehicles retain wheels. Bodies hop/rock and land at both movement endpoints while shadows and sorting stay planted. Scoop work retains its .4-second dip/dirt puff. Construction, dismantling and destruction have a .7-second smoke billow revealing parts beneath it; pause freezes the puff and reduced motion uses a .25-second static puff. Flag cloth flutters, reward stars rise with a chime, questions bob, and earned rovers patrol the map. Pause and reduced-motion behavior remain in place. The ocean missions feature playable boats; completed boat-bearing missions earn appropriate water residents on Open Sea. Water actors use the lower water plane, with continuous shoreline height and rendered-anchor picking.

Scoop shows only Move, Dig and Fill. Bucket state and charge disable unavailable terrain actions; pickup/drop are rejected by targeting and simulation APIs. Selecting a relay shows its recoverable parts and Take apart, with rover actions hidden. Other cargo rovers retain travelling pickup/drop. Routine movement stays silent; success and reward messages dismiss after 3.5 seconds. Invalid actions, including a map command without a selected rover, clear any preceding transient message and play the existing error cue without a popup, honoring effects mute.

An active action names itself above the map with a Cancel button. Cargo and terrain modes mark legal targets and accept distant reachable targets. A travelling order shows the approach route and a separate marker on its action target. Build mode marks all nine tiles in the 3×3 supply area, shows local availability/missing materials, and warns when a valid rover build will use an empty battery. Invalid commands preserve resources and existing orders. Space follows the catalog: Scoop Dig/Fill, Arborbot Uproot/Plant, Dozer Push, or cargo pickup/drop. Passive support, creatures, and automatic fighters need no extra action button. Escape cancels the mode. Successful actions return to movement.

Loose-part count badges appear only on hover, with no duplicate text panel. Ground battery strips remain visible. Rock clusters are centered on their full support footprint. Water renders beneath the raised island; each land/bridge tile paints its sides and top together, back to front. Rear-facing atlas pictures are mapped to their actual screen headings, correcting upper-left and upper-right motion for every character.

## Audio

The original 64-second **Little Expeditions** theme uses a bouncy retro arrangement: square-wave lead, synth bass, syncopated keyboard chords, marimba and light drums, with contrasting phrases and a quieter ending. Its editable score and offline renderer are in [render-music.py](../scripts/render-music.py); instrument samples use the [MIT-licensed FluidR3 soundfont](../public/FLUIDR3-LICENSE.txt). A quiet synthesized electrical motor follows actual rover movement; cargo transfers, construction, dismantling, battery replacement, hits, wrecks, errors, signals, and mission completion retain their distinct short cues. Queued cargo actions play their transfer cue only after a successful arrival, independent of the selected rover.

Music and effects have separate pictured controls inside Game menu. Both start off on every game load, including when older saved data says on; enabling either is an opt-in for that tab's current session. Audio settings are not persisted. The first trusted click or keypress activates Web Audio; activation alone does not enable either channel. Only opting into music downloads its approximately 1 MB stereo recording, served with the game. Pausing stops motor audio and lowers enabled music; hiding the tab suspends playback and discards transient effects. Resuming, restarting, and changing mute settings do not duplicate loops. A music load failure leaves effects and gameplay available and can be retried by enabling music again.

## Explicit movement rules

- Four-neighbor movement on a square logical grid, presented isometrically.
- Each complete route uses the kind's mobility profile. Land includes grass, sand, swamp, and boardwalks; Trailbuggy/Mender also cross rough ground. Frog/Duck add shallow water to land; Fish/boats use shallow/deep water only. Scorpion/Rex use rough profiles. Gator uses land and both water types but excludes rough; deep access remains a provisional adaptation because reference accounts disagree. Trees, rocks, relays, and occupied/reserved tiles block movement. Loose parts do not block travel.
- Scout is faster than Hauler. Orders persist when selection changes.
- Replacement and stop orders finish the active edge without teleporting.
- Occupied/reserved tiles cannot be entered or built upon. Routes replan or wait when construction or altered terrain blocks a path. One-wide head-on deadlocks may require redirection.
- Camera movement is explicit. Focus and Overview are separate actions.

## Explicit construction rules

- Materials: red, blue, yellow and green parts, physical batteries with individual remaining charge, and physical dirt loads. Dirt is cargo for terrain work, not a recipe ingredient. No global wallet.
- Scout carries 2, Hauler 4, Warden 1, Trailbuggy 3, Forklift 10, Bulk hauler 25, Tugboat 5, and Freighter 25 cargo items. Mixed cargo is allowed for these units. Pickup loads as much as fits: red, blue, yellow and green parts, then batteries, then dirt, leaving excess on the ground. Scoop holds one dirt load through Dig/Fill, without cargo pickup/drop. Cargo units’ drop-off unloads all cargo and merges with an existing pile.
- Cargo orders can target reachable clear tiles, including shore supplies for boats; approaches use the carrier's terrain profile. The rover chooses a shortest reachable approach among the four orthogonal neighbors, travels there, then attempts the transfer. It may replan its approach if blocked. Arrival revalidates the target and energy; failure keeps the cargo and reports why. Selecting another object preserves the order; Stop or a valid replacement order cancels it. Merely entering or cancelling targeting does not stop a previous order.
- Distant drop-off follows the user’s reference clarification. Applying the same behavior to pickup is a deliberate choice for our game.
- Recipes: Scout = 1 yellow + 1 green + 1 battery; Hauler = 2 red + 1 blue + 1 battery; Warden = 1 red + 2 blue + 1 battery; Scoop = 1 red + 1 blue + 2 yellow + 1 battery; Signal relay = 1 red + 1 blue + 2 green. An empty battery is valid for construction and produces a powerless rover. Available roles and finite counts are authored per mission in the canonical registry and its imported content files. All current recipes live in [catalog.ts](../src/core/catalog.ts). Every mobile kind and Sentry uses one battery; Charging station, Bot workshop, and Marina use none.
- Building uses only loose parts in the full 3×3 area centered on the site, including all four diagonals. Carried supplies do not count. The highest-charge available battery is used for a rover. The preview and committed build share the same validation.
- Construction is immediate on a clear compatible tile: boats/Fish use water, Marina needs shallow water, land units follow their profile, and Signal relay requires grass/sand. No nearby builder is required. `Mission.blueprints` authors finite per-model stock: a successful build spends one, a failed build spends none, and zero-stock choices are disabled. The smoke puff is presentation, not a construction timer.
- Dismantling returns 100% of the recipe, the installed battery with its remaining charge, and any carried cargo to the object's tile. Moving rovers must stop first. Dismantling and destruction never refund a blueprint; rebuilding needs remaining stock and creates no battery charge.
- Transfers, construction, and dismantling conserve material/battery counts across loose piles, cargo, and built objects. Automatic support intentionally restores installed charge, and roster enemies release authored salvage. Transfers and rebuilds preserve the charge of batteries carried as parts. Only the acting rover’s installed battery supplies energy; rebuilding cannot refill it. Failed local transfers do not spend action energy, although travel already performed still costs energy.
- Pausing freezes movement, enemy decisions, combat and presentation puffs, while nearby cargo/terrain actions and construction remain available for stationary rovers. Distant orders wait for movement to resume. Restart restores a fresh instance of the current mission, including authored terrain, enemies, resources, blueprint stock and objective history. Choosing an unlocked mission on the world map starts fresh; saved main/bonus awards remain, but that visit's main must complete before its bonus unlocks.

## Explicit terrain-work rules

- Scoop is unarmed and moves at 1.55 tiles per second. Dig requires an empty bucket and changes one open grass/sand tile into water, gaining 1 dirt. Fill requires that dirt and changes one water tile into grass, consuming the load. Boardwalks, trees, and rocks cannot be dug.
- Work requires a stopped Scoop on a directly orthogonal neighbor. Distant commands choose a reachable approach, travel, then revalidate terrain, occupancy, protected sites, cargo, and charge before acting. Selection preserves orders; Stop or a valid replacement cancels them. Invalid targets leave existing orders intact.
- Work protects current/next unit and enemy cells, relays, reserved friendly routes, loose piles, goal cells, and bonus pads. Routes avoid newly blocked terrain.
- Land plus carried/loose dirt is conserved. Scoop releases dirt through dismantling or destruction; cargo rovers can haul loose dirt. Scoop cannot pick it back up. Rebuilding with a dead installed battery preserves its zero charge. Each simulation clones the authored grid; restart/replay restore terrain and do not save in-progress mutations.

- Dozer pushes a rock or entire loose pile one tile away from its working side onto grass/sand/swamp. Loose piles can combine with an existing pile, preserving every colored part, battery charge and dirt load; rocks require an empty destination. Distant Push chooses a reachable side with a legal destination and revalidates both on arrival. Protected sites, reserved routes, occupied tiles, and loose trees prevent the push.
- Arborbot lifts one planted or loose tree and replants on clear grass/sand/swamp, moving slower while loaded. It preserves the ground under uprooted trees and conserves tree count. Dismantling or destruction releases a carried tree for recovery without blocking wreck supplies. Distant Uproot/Plant revalidates on arrival.

## Provisional battery balance

- Installed capacity: 100 charge. Each mission authors starting charge, including partial and empty batteries in power/rescue challenges.
- Travel costs 1 per ordinary entered tile and 3 for swamp, paid before starting the edge. The final charge still finishes that edge, then the rover stops at a tile center. No energy is spent while idle or waiting for a blocked route.
- Each successful pickup/drop costs 2. A rover without enough charge cannot perform that action. An order that exhausts its energy stops, reports the failure, and retains cargo.
- Each successful Dig/Fill/Push/Uproot/Plant costs 3. Rejected work spends no action charge; any travel already performed still costs charge.
- Replace battery is an explicit player action on a stopped rover. It uses the best battery with more charge from loose supplies in the surrounding 3×3 area and returns the old one to the rover’s tile. It does not require power from the depleted rover and creates no charge. Battery delivery by another rover therefore enables recovery.
- Each successful Warden/Patrol boat/Sentry hit costs 1 charge. Enemy hits remove charge equal to their damage. An empty battery prevents attacking as well as movement.
- Spending the final charge through movement or actions leaves an intact, powerless rover. A damaging enemy hit that reduces charge to zero, or hits a rover already at zero, destroys it. Wreckage includes an empty installed battery; carried batteries retain their charge. Dismantling remains different: it preserves the installed battery's remaining charge.
- Automatic adjacent support pulses every second, restoring up to 6 charge. Charging station serves vehicles, Bot workshop bots, and Marina boats, including depleted matching units. Buildings service all adjacent matching units from external power. Mender serves one adjacent bot per pulse and spends 1 own charge. Creatures and Sentry are outside these recharge families.
- Battery-free support buildings take structural integrity damage and release recipe parts when destroyed; Sentry remains battery-powered.
- These numeric values, support rates, and replacement interactions are choices for our prototype, not verified values or mechanics of the original.

## Hollow Reach: Shore, then Ridge power

Starting conditions: one fully charged Scout, one fully charged Hauler, 1 red, 1 blue and 2 green loose parts. Those two supply piles are adjacent to Hauler. An empty battery sits near the starting supplies; a spare full battery lies further northeast on the western island. Two fixed crossings connect the islands. Shore at (15,11) is the single main flag. One relay, one Hauler and one Scout blueprint are available.

Goal: move a friendly mobile unit onto Shore. Arrival at the tile center records it, including when the unit uses its final charge or passes through on the way elsewhere. The flag stays reached after it leaves; hostile creatures and static relays do not count. Main completion unlocks Parts and Paths and reveals Ridge power at (15,2): deliver one charged battery. The starting Hauler can recover the spare battery for this optional bonus. Construction and hauling remain available, subject to finite blueprint stock.

## Bramble Crossing: reach the eastern shore

A safe western camp contains Scout, Hauler, 1 red, 1 blue and 2 green for optional construction, and a separate Warden kit of 1 red, 2 blue and a full battery. A two-tile-wide boardwalk connects it to the eastern island. One Bristleback patrols near the far end; the East flag lies beyond it. The camp stays outside detection range while the player prepares.

The suggested route builds Warden beside the kit, moves it toward the crossing to draw and defeat the enemy, then sends a rover onto East at (17,8). The main requires both arrival and all hostiles cleared. Completion reveals the eastern pad at (17,10), requiring 1 red, 1 blue and 2 green. A single rover battery gauge, enemy health gauge, short impact flashes, and destruction messages communicate the encounter without an attack button or per-hit text.

Provisional combat rules:

- All friendly rovers use their physical battery's charge as endurance, with a full capacity of 100. Scout and Hauler are unarmed. Warden hits for 4 damage every 0.7 seconds and moves at 1.9 tiles per second.
- Bristleback has 20 health, hits for 4 damage every 0.9 seconds, and moves at 1.75 tiles per second.
- Creatures wander using seeded patrol choices or follow an authored circuit. Within their detection range (normally four steps), they prefer powered fighters over unarmed units, with adjacent fighters taking first priority. A fighter needs enough installed charge for another attack; its attack cooldown does not remove that priority. Among equally threatening targets, a creature keeps its current chase up to its pursuit limit (normally six steps), then chooses the nearest candidate. Switching targets does not reset attack or notice timers. After losing all targets it resumes its patrol; routes respect occupied and reserved tiles.
- Opponents attack automatically within one orthogonal step of their actual positions, with a small movement tolerance; diagonal tile centers are out of range. Selection and player orders do not enable or disable attacks. Cooldowns prevent continuous damage. Simultaneous lethal hits can destroy both opponents.
- Destroyed rovers return their entire recipe, carried cargo, and an empty installed battery to the ground. A rover destroyed between tiles drops at the nearest endpoint. Rebuilding never restores charge: a wreck's empty battery builds a powerless rover unless another charged battery is available nearby. Charged batteries carried as cargo remain usable. Bristleback drops no parts; the six roster hostiles release authored colored parts and one charged battery, as listed in [ROSTER.md](ROSTER.md).

These values establish the first playable encounter and need playtesting. The roster extension adds larger encounters and support systems; balance remains provisional.

## Siltwater Reach: make a crossing

The 16×12 mission starts with a fully charged Scoop at (4,6) and Scout at (3,6) on a broad west bank. A two-cell river at x8–9 separates them from the Far bank flag at (13,6). Borrow two spare land tiles and fill successive river cells from the walkable edge; preserve a route back to the crossing, then send a rover onto the flag. No enemies start here.

A kit at (5,8) contains 1 red, 1 blue, 2 yellow and a charged battery; extras at (4,9) contain 4 red and 2 blue. One blueprint each for Scoop, Hauler, Scout and relay is available. Main completion reveals the optional far-bank pad at (12,9), which requires 2 red and 1 blue. Coordinates are zero-based. Completing this mission adds one Scoop map resident through the shared reward pipeline.

## World map and completion

The game opens on the first incomplete world's landscape. Meadow Isles, Sunstone Range and Open Sea each show twelve ordered locations, with previous/next controls beside the world name and a per-world completion count. Only Hollow starts unlocked on a fresh save; all preceding main awards are required, including across world boundaries. Future landscapes can be browsed without unlocking their missions. Yellow question marks mark available locations; dark stationary locks and muted paths mark inaccessible ones. Completed flags carry earned bonus stars. Names appear on hover/focus. Every completed mission adds one small matching resident on its own map, and boat scenery belongs only to completed boat-bearing ocean missions. Changing worlds or replaying does not duplicate residents. Map and mission Overview cameras fit their actual grid dimensions. Mission visits also initially fit the whole board at desktop widths of at least 700px, including the larger final boards.

Completing the main shows a rising star with a chime. The star freezes the simulation and lifts with cubic ease-out for 1.25 seconds before the native modal dialog opens; reduced motion uses a static 0.35-second reward. Clicking the canvas, Space from the game surface, or Escape skips the final beat; Space still activates focused native buttons. Completion and newly earned bonuses publish in the event frame and save immediately, including when returning to the map during the reward. The mission pauses while the dialog is open. **World map** returns to the landscape; **Try bonus** (or Escape) restores the previous pause state and resumes that run. The main popup appears once per run, including replays. Completing the bonus uses the same frozen star reward, then opens **Bonus complete** with a starred flag, **Bonus star earned** and only **World map**; Escape returns to the map and this final run cannot resume. Each replay gets this ending even when its star was already saved, without a duplicate award. Returning from the top-bar map button before completion does not mark the mission complete.

Each visit's bonus marker, ground star and text remain hidden and ineligible until that visit's main completes, even with prior saved awards. Hollow requires a charged battery at Ridge (15,2), Bramble requires 1 red, 1 blue and 2 green at (17,10), and Siltwater requires 2 red and 1 blue at (12,9). Rough and Tidepool instead require arrival by their original Snail and Duck. Deposits are checked on the marked tile; supplies remain recoverable and a star is awarded once per visit. A dead battery does not qualify for a power delivery. Main completion is sufficient to unlock the next mission. Bonuses save when earned after **Try bonus**.

Progress stores known completed mission IDs and earned bonus IDs in local browser storage. Reloading opens the map with those flags; every mission visit starts a fresh simulation with authored stock. Existing known awards remain, but a noncontiguous award cannot bypass a missing preceding main. Refresh replaces local awards with the saved record, and new awards read that record before saving, so a stale tab cannot restore cleared awards or add a bonus without its completion. Game menu shows **Restart level** during a mission: clicking starts that mission fresh and preserves saved awards. On the world map, the same control becomes **Reset ALL progress**, requiring a fresh continuous three-second primary pointer, Space, or Enter hold; short map clicks and interrupted holds do nothing. A screen change cancels the hold. Confirmation clears completions and bonus stars and removes earned map residents. Storage failure retains session progress. Returning to the map stops mission simulation and rover motors while preserving the audio settings.

The landscape and mission share one Phaser canvas, with separate scenes that sleep between visits. Screen transitions place the canvas before native overlays, keeping mission buttons and camera controls above it. The central transition refreshes the new parent's bounds before the scale refresh; the world camera also sizes itself before centering, so native pins and canvas use the current host dimensions. Pictured mission buttons and the completion dialog are native DOM controls with keyboard focus. Map pins and name labels use blurred shadows; the borderless completion dialog uses a diffuse shadow over its dimmed backdrop and a yellow **World map** action. Restart clears the current run without clearing saved completion flags.
